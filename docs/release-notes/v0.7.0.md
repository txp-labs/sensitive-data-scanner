## What 0.7.0 is

Every run now says what its findings **may mean**: which rules and laws the
data it found may bring into scope, what leaving that data in place may mean,
and who decides. The report has a new section, *What these findings may
mean*, right after the summary, and the findings document carries the same
hints as `scopeHints` (findings schema 1.15). Slack scanning gains an opt-in
auto-join and findings that open the message itself.

**These are hints, never a verdict, and not legal advice.** They come only
from the kinds of data found, not from what your business does.

### What you get

- **PCI DSS** when card data is found, with the systems it may reach and how
  removing or masking the data keeps them out. A card verification code or
  PIN adds that PCI DSS never allows keeping it after authorization.
- **US breach-notification laws** when Social Security numbers, ITINs,
  account or card numbers are found, with the FTC Safeguards Rule for a
  financial institution.
- **HIPAA only when you say the deployment holds health information**, with
  the new setting `HEALTH_DATA` (AWS: the `HealthData` parameter, `off` by
  default). The scanner detects no health information, so it never suggests
  HIPAA on its own; with it off, a report holding Social Security numbers or
  dates of birth says how to turn it on.
- Each hint counts the locations behind it and how many are **not encrypted
  at rest**.
- **Slack:** `SLACK_JOIN_PUBLIC_CHANNELS` (off by default) joins public
  channels before reading them, with a manifest for each choice; findings
  link to the message, and every SaaS deploy example reads the Slack token
  from your own secret store.

### What it does not do

- It does not decide whether a framework applies. Your assessor, privacy
  officer or counsel does.
- `HEALTH_DATA` is a parameter of the AWS template only. The Azure, Google
  Cloud, SaaS and databases runners read it from their environment.
- A hint never holds a value: its words are fixed text and its numbers are
  counts.
